\section{Model and Stateful Protocol}\label{sec:fresh-updates}\label{sec:fresh-model}
We study an application that maintains a matrix product as new low-rank
updates arrive. The application retains its earlier result; the processor
may retain arbitrary paid state. A round has two ordering requirements:
the bilinear program is selected before the new factors, and the complete
candidate is fixed before the verifier's random vectors
(Figure~\ref{fig:fresh-flow}).


\subsection{Inputs, state, and computation}
Fix a finite field $\F_q$ with $q$ elements, integers $n\ge r\ge2$, an
initial $A_0\in\F_q^{n\times n}$, and $B\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\F_q)$, the
group of invertible $n\times n$ matrices over $\F_q$.
The authenticated initial product is $C_0=A_0B$. Write
$\xleftarrow{\$}$ for an independent uniform draw. The intended
trajectory of accepted updates then satisfies, in round $j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-task}
\begin{gathered}
U_j,V_j\xleftarrow{\$}\F_q^{n\times r},\qquad
A_j=A_{j-1}+U_jV_j^\top,\\
C_j=A_jB=C_{j-1}+U_j(V_j^\top B).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Uniformity includes rank-deficient factors. Let $\mathcal H_j^-$ be the
complete classical history just before disclosure: earlier factors,
outputs, checking vectors, all suppliers' state, the selected program,
its gate allocation, and the coins used to select them. Conditional on
every such history, $U_j$ and $V_j$ remain independent and uniform.
Section~\ref{sec:escape} gives a conditional nonuniform extension.

Preprocessing can depend arbitrarily on $A_0,B$, and earlier inputs. Its
actual cost $P$ is charged, and finite aggregate state $S$ includes all
advice, tables, circuits, and caches held by all suppliers. The theorem
places no numerical bound on $P$ or $S$: it constrains the representation
and timing of the computation that uses the new factors.

\begin{definition}[Admitted online program]\label{def:fresh-program}
Conditional on $\mathcal H_j^-$, the increment is one fixed homogeneous
bilinear program: with scalar linear forms $L_{j,a},R_{j,a}$ in the
entries of $U,V$, output coefficient matrices
$Z_{j,a}\in\F_q^{n\times n}$, and gate count $w_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-program}
F_j(U,V)=\sum_{a=1}^{w_j}Z_{j,a}L_{j,a}(U)R_{j,a}(V).
\end{equation}
The linear forms, output coefficients, topology, and gate count are fixed
before the current factors arrive. A gate multiplies one linear
form in each fresh factor group. The count includes every supplier's
bilinear gates contributing to the round; unused gates may have zero
output coefficients.
\end{definition}

The fixed transform $V^\top B$ is linear in fresh inputs. Its field
operations, state access, additions, communication, and output
materialization retain their actual execution costs, but are not the
variable-by-variable gates counted by $w_j$. Exact fast bilinear
algorithms are admitted. Input-indexed program selection, branching on
current inputs, and nonlinear encodings of those inputs are distinct
computational models~\cite{alman_lookup}. Paid state may select the next
program before disclosure; an answer computed from the current input by
another supplier must be charged as current computation.

\subsection{Candidate and verification}
The verifier stores a reference $\widehat C_{j-1}$, initially $C_0$.
The candidate is
$\widetilde C_j=\widehat C_{j-1}+F_j(U_j,V_j)$.
Its entire actual matrix is fixed before the verifier draws $t_j\ge1$
independent uniform vectors $z_{j,h}\in\F_q^n$, conditional on the
history, factors, and candidate, where $[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ for positive
integers $m$. The verifier tests
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-check}
(\widetilde C_j-\widehat C_{j-1})z_{j,h}
=U_j\bigl(V_j^\top(Bz_{j,h})\bigr),\quad h\in[t_j].
\end{equation}
Acceptance commits $A_j$ and advances $\widehat C_j$ to the candidate.
Rejection commits neither update and ends the prescribed sequence.
Recovery starts a new sequence from an authenticated, consistent checkpoint,
with its own work budget and acceptance event. Failed candidates and
transcripts may remain in paid state; freshness in a restarted sequence
is conditioned on this full history. The accepted reference alone serves
as the previous-product reference. A fully received matrix
satisfies the candidate boundary; a cryptographic representation must
bind a complete matrix and account for its binding error.

We consider $k$ prescribed attempts with a \emph{pathwise} budget
$\sum_{j=1}^k w_j\le W$. Choices may adapt to the complete observed
history. Aborting before round $k$ fails the all-execute-and-accept event;
selecting successes from unbounded retries is a different event.

A concrete matrix view accumulates cross-products of paired feature
streams. A batch of $r$ paired $n$-dimensional records forms the columns
of $U_j,V_j$; the view maintains
$A_j=A_0+\sum_{i\le j}U_iV_i^\top$, and fixed $B$ transforms its right-hand
features. The uniform model describes independently sampled finite-field
records. Independent coordinates with bounded conditional maximum atoms
give the nonuniform instance of Corollary~\ref{cor:coordinate-escape}.
These are assumptions on the incoming records, rather than consequences
of requesting the product. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} also measures a
structured row-replacement view through the same update interface.

\section{Fresh Computation and Acceptance Bounds}\label{sec:fresh-theory}
We first establish the tensor threshold and a history-conditioned
single-round bound. Corollary~\ref{ext:cor-work-acceptance} refines this
bound as a function of work, and Theorem~\ref{ext:thm-profile-dp} composes
the resulting profiles across adaptive rounds.
Theorems~\ref{ext:thm-balanced} and~\ref{ext:thm-heterogeneous} then allocate
a fixed budget of checking vectors.

Let $\mathcal R_q(n,r,n)$ be the tensor rank of the $\langle n,r,n\rangle$
multiplication map $(U,Y)\mapsto UY$, with $U\in\F_q^{n\times r}$ and
$Y\in\F_q^{r\times n}$: the least number of simple terms, each the product
of one linear form in $U$, one linear form in $Y$, and a fixed output
coefficient matrix. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-parameters}
R=\mathcal R_q(n,r,n),\qquad
\theta_q=2/q-1/q^2=1-(1-1/q)^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Fresh-input tensor]\label{lem:fresh-tensor}
The map $T_B(U,V)=U(V^\top B)$ is linearly isomorphic to the
$\langle n,r,n\rangle$ multiplication tensor. Its tensor rank is
$R\ge n^2$. For any fixed pre-input state, an admitted program exact on
all inputs uses at least $R$ bilinear gates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The input change $\Phi_B(V)=V^\top B$ is invertible, with inverse
$\Phi_B^{-1}(Y)=(YB^{-1})^\top$. Tensor rank is invariant under this
change, while each summand of~\eqref{eq:fresh-program} is a simple
input--input--output tensor. Conditioning on $\mathcal H_j^-$ makes
all coefficients constants relative to the new inputs, however they were
prepared, so the program tensor has rank at most $w_j$.

For the explicit lower bound, output flattening sends a functional
$\lambda$ to $(U,Y)\mapsto\lambda(UY)$. If this form vanishes,
the choices $U=e_i e_1^\top$ and $Y=e_1 e_\ell^\top$, where $e_i$ is
the $i$-th standard basis vector and $E_{i\ell}=e_ie_\ell^\top$ is the
corresponding matrix unit, imply $\lambda(E_{i\ell})=0$ for every
$i,\ell$. The flattening is thus
injective on an $n^2$-dimensional space. A simple tensor has flattening
rank at most one, proving $R\ge n^2$. These sparse inputs are algebraic
witnesses at the same factor width $r$.

Over a finite field, equality of bilinear functions determines their
tensors: evaluation on pairs of input basis vectors recovers every
coefficient. Hence $w_j<R$ implies $F_j-T_B$ is a nonzero bilinear
map as a tensor and as a function.
\end{proof}

Fast rectangular algorithms can reduce the direct $n^2r$ gate count,
while every exact admitted program still needs at least $R\ge n^2$
gates. This argument counts computation, separately from writing the
$n^2$ output entries.

\begin{theorem}[One-round exactness and acceptance]\label{thm:fresh-one}
Fix a history $\mathcal H_j^-$ and a program with $w_j<R$. Its
increment agrees with $T_B$ with probability at most $\theta_q$.
With $t$ checking vectors its acceptance probability is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-w2}
a(t)=\theta_q+(1-\theta_q)q^{-t}.
\end{equation}
If the preceding reference is genuine, the increment-agreement event
is exactly $\widetilde C_j=C_j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero coordinate of $F_j-T_B$ is $p(u,v)=u^\top Mv$, where $u=\operatorname{vec}(U)$ and
$v=\operatorname{vec}(V)$ stack the entries of $U$ and $V$ in a fixed
order, and $\operatorname{rank}(M)=s\ge1$. Uniformity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-nonzero}
\Prb[p(u,v)\ne0\mid\mathcal H_j^-]
=(1-q^{-s})(1-q^{-1})\ge(1-q^{-1})^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $M^\top u\ne0$ with probability $1-q^{-s}$; conditional
on such a $u$, its inner product with independent uniform $v$ is
uniform in $\F_q$. Full increment agreement implies $p=0$.

Write $D=F_j(U_j,V_j)-T_B(U_j,V_j)$. The candidate and the checker
use the same previous reference, so~\eqref{eq:fresh-check} tests $Dz=0$
even if that reference is wrong. After candidate fixation, nonzero $D$
passes $t$ independent vectors with probability
$q^{-t\operatorname{rank}(D)}\le q^{-t}$~\cite{freivalds}.
For increment-agreement probability $p_j\le\theta_q$, total
acceptance is at most $p_j+(1-p_j)q^{-t}\le a(t)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform factors and singular fixed operands}
For uniform factors and $t_j=t$, put
$b=\min\{k,\lfloor W/R\rfloor\}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:fresh-stateful} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh-w3-uniform}
\Prb[\text{all $k$ attempts execute and accept}]
\le a(t)^{k-b}.
\end{equation}
This holds for adaptive programs and allocations under the pathwise
budget. It is one when $b=k$. The work-sensitive profile below retains
the individual gate counts instead of only this expensive-round count.

If $\operatorname{rank}(B)=s$, write
$B=P\operatorname{diag}(I_s,0)Q$ with invertible $P,Q$, where $I_s$ is the
$s\times s$ identity matrix. The input change $V'=P^\top V$ and output
change $C\mapsto CQ^{-1}$ give
$(U,V')\mapsto[U(V'_{1:s,:})^\top\;0]$, where $V'_{1:s,:}$ denotes the
first $s$ rows of $V'$.
Restriction and projection recover $\langle n,r,s\rangle$, and
embedding gives the reverse reduction. The fresh tensor rank is therefore
$\mathcal R_q(n,r,s)\ge ns$, by the same output flattening. At
$B=0$ the increment is zero. The main workload uses invertible $B$.
